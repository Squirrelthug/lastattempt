!!! know "What Blizzard confirmed"
    Beta September 17 – October 22 (level 30 cap). Early name reservation and character creation October 27 for Heroic Pack owners. Global launch November 4. Raids unlock December 9: Barrow Deeps (10), Hyjal Summit (20), Onyxia's Lair (40). Hardcore launches in winter as its own ruleset. Spring: two new raids, two dungeons, new quests and playable area, a new Legendary questline, PvP season refresh. Summer: a revamped iconic raid, a new raid, expanded world content, two more dungeons, another PvP refresh, professions and Legacy updates.

The World of Warcraft: Forever beta opened September 17 and closes October 22. Five days later, on October 27, players who bought the right version get early name reservation and character creation. On November 4, everyone, everywhere, logs in for free and picks a ruleset — PvP, PvE, RP, and later, at the end of winter, Hardcore.

Five weeks after that, December 9, the raids unlock: a 10-man, a 20-man and a 40-man. They aren't flexible. They don't scale up or down. You bring at most 10, 20 and 40 players. The friction of finding enough people for a 40, or splitting your guild into smaller groups for the 10, is part of the fun you find in this version of the game.

## Then the cadence starts

Spring brings more raids and dungeons, new quests and playable area, and a new Legendary — nobody has any idea what it is yet. Summer brings more major updates, including a revamped iconic raid. My guess, honestly, is Molten Core. We'll see. More world content, two more dungeons.

And there's a pattern in there: PvP refreshes. Each major Forever patch looks like it brings a new PvP season and a resetting ladder. There's a lot of focus on PvP in this version of the game, and I think a lot of PvPers may find themselves enjoying Forever more than mainline WoW.

## What Hardcore needed

There's a deeply dedicated community around Hardcore, and the realmless setup is going to make it much easier to keep those players together, regardless of when they jump in or out. I expect a lot of streamers joining those guilds to broadcast their Hardcore runs and reach new heights.

But remember how the last wave went. There are only so many times you can run Molten Core. Hardcore basically ran out of content, and the movement fizzled — plenty of people still play it, but far fewer stream Hardcore raids and Hardcore content than did at the peak.

In a game that continuously expands horizontally, that problem goes away. You can build characters to a level cap that is not a moving goalpost, and comfortably switch between the alts you've spent time levelling to approach whatever Blizzard releases next. I think that's extremely healthy for the Hardcore community, and exactly the answer that version of the game needed.

Tim Jones called Hardcore the purest form of Classic anyone can play, and I genuinely agree. There's something about the tension in every fight, every time you step into a cave not knowing if you'll walk out. It's a completely different emotion, and it demands a much more attentive player. Going AFK in the middle of the forest will not serve you well here.

!!! open "What the roadmap leaves open"
    What the revamped iconic raid is. What the Legendary is. And whether Hardcore, arriving after launch into a game with new content on the way can keeps its momentum this time.

Beyond Hardcore, Forever is shaping up as a genuinely exciting roadmap laid out across 2026 and 2027. The community is watching where it goes, and so are we — we'll report on updates as they land.
